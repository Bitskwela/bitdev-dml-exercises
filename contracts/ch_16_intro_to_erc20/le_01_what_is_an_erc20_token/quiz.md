# Lesson 1 Quiz: What Is an ERC-20 Token?

---
# Quiz 1
## Scenario: The Notebook That Couldn't Prove Anything

Kevin says he earned 40 WCR for last week's exercise. Dan's spiral notebook is smudged — the number could be 40 or 10 — and it's the only record, readable and editable only by Dan.

**Question 1:** Hype aside, what is the *core* of an ERC-20 token?
A. A coin you buy low and sell high for profit
B. A ledger of who-holds-how-much — a list of addresses and balances — living in a program on a blockchain
C. A unique digital collectible, like a profile-picture NFT
D. A physical notebook backed up to the cloud

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Strip away the hype and a token is just a shared ledger of balances — the sari-sari utang listahan, turned into code. "ERC-20" is the standard shape it follows; the token is the balances themselves.

---

**Question 2:** Why couldn't Dan's notebook settle the dispute, even though he's honest?
A. The notebook used the wrong currency symbol
B. He is the single source of truth — only he can read and edit it — so every dispute reduces to "trust Dan's memory," and memory isn't proof
C. Notebooks are illegal for tracking credits
D. Kevin never actually finished the exercise

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** The flaw isn't that it's paper — it's that only Dan can read and edit it. That makes him the single point of failure: an honest mistake and a quiet edit look identical to the kids.

---

**Question 3:** What does **fungible** mean, and why does it fit workshop credits?
A. Every unit is unique, so you care which one you get
B. Every unit is identical and interchangeable (and divisible) — any 40 WCR equals any other 40 WCR — which is exactly what credits should be
C. The token can never be divided into smaller parts
D. The token's price is fixed by law

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Fungible = interchangeable and divisible. Credit #17 and #932 are worth the same; a credit is a credit. That's the opposite of an NFT, where token #7 differs from #8.

# Quiz 2
## Scenario: "Scam yan"

Tita Malou hears the word "token" and says *"scam yan, nakita ko sa Facebook."* Jasper, meanwhile, says Dan should mint a PFP collection instead.

**Question 4:** What is the honest answer to Tita Malou's scam worry about WCR?
A. WCR will definitely increase in value over time
B. WCR is a transparent points system you earn and spend on turon — nobody buys, sells, or invests in it; its value is *transparency*, not profit
C. She should invest a small amount first to see what happens
D. All tokens are in fact scams, so she's right

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** WCR is a loyalty-points ledger that happens to be tamper-proof. The answer to "scam yan" is always transparency, never profit — a hard rule of this course.

---

**Question 5:** Why was Jasper's "mint a PFP collection" advice wrong for the workshop?
A. PFPs are too cheap to bother with
B. A PFP collection is non-fungible — each token is unique — but credits must be interchangeable and splittable, so an NFT is the opposite of what Dan needs
C. Jasper's collection wasn't on Ethereum
D. There's no difference; both would work equally well

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Workshop credits need to be fungible: any 40 equals any other 40. A one-of-a-kind collectible you'd care about losing is exactly the wrong shape for points.

---

**Question 6:** Moving the credits from a notebook to a token ledger changes what, exactly?
A. It changes what the credits are worth in pesos
B. It changes *who can see them* and *who can change them* — not what they are; the credits are still a list of who-holds-how-much
C. It makes the credits impossible to spend
D. It hands full control of the credits to Jasper

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Moving the same listahan to an ERC-20 doesn't change what a credit is — it changes who can read it (everyone) and who can edit it (only the token's own rules, recorded as transactions).
